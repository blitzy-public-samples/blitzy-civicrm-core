# STORY-003 — Recurring-gift receipt viewing

The third story of the Epic defined in [`epics.md`](epics.md), which also carries the story
roster, the JIRA import reconciliation and the consolidated clarification summary. This document
carries the nine mandated story fields and nothing else — there is no Epic field among them, by
design.

This story **implements** the four-predicate scoping contract published once in
[`STORY-001-donor-identity-and-self-only-scoping.md`](STORY-001-donor-identity-and-self-only-scoping.md);
it does not restate it. Where a predicate is referred to below it is referred to by its number in
that contract, so a change to the rule is made in one place and cannot leave this path behind. It
is reached from the row-level link supplied by
[`STORY-002-recurring-contribution-history-list.md`](STORY-002-recurring-contribution-history-list.md),
which provides the affordance; this story provides what the affordance resolves to.

## Story ID

## Title

Recurring-gift receipt viewing

---

## Description

Let a donor open the receipt for one of their own recurring-gift contributions, reached from the
row-level link on the STORY-002 list.

**The read.** A purpose-scoped server-side path gates on the donor role's permission, then issues
an APIv4 `Contribution.get` with `checkPermissions` set false and **all four of STORY-001's
predicates appended by the server**. The caller supplies a contribution identifier and nothing
that can widen the query. This mirrors the list path deliberately, so that the record-level
boundary lives in one kind of place on both donor surfaces rather than in two different
mechanisms a reviewer has to compare.

**That design is forced rather than preferred**, and the distinction matters: a reader who takes
it for a preference will reasonably propose the safer-*looking* alternative of leaving permission
checks on. Core requires `access CiviCRM` **and** `access CiviContribute` for a permission-checked
`Contribution.get`, and `access CiviContribute` itself confers viewing all contributions for
visible contacts — so it is over-privileged for a donor, and a permission-checked read is
therefore unavailable to a least-privileged donor role. The addresses are in Technical Notes.

**The consequence is explicit and must not be discovered later.** With checks disabled, neither
the generic Contact ACL clause nor the financial-type filter is inherited. Any financial-type
restriction on donor receipts has to be applied by this path itself, because it is inherited from
nowhere — which is `CLR-05`.

**This story also owns the reuse-or-replace decision.** A donor-reachable receipt route already
ships in core, `civicrm/contribute/invoice`, and its access check does not verify that the
requested contribution belongs to the caller. Whether to harden that route and reuse it, or leave
it as it is and specify an independent receipt path, is `CLR-10` — raised here and deliberately
not answered.

The read stays in the API layer: **no BAO or DAO call is introduced on this path**, per the
instruction governing this Epic.

---

## Business Value

Answers the request donors currently have to make of staff.

The business objective supplied with this request records that **a donor who wants a copy of a
past receipt has to ask staff for it** — that specific request is the one this story removes from
the queue. It also records that **every request goes through a staff member** who reads the
donor's record directly, that fielding them **costs the team several hours a week** across the
people who handle them, and that **donors are frustrated at being unable to do simple things
themselves outside business hours**, because the queue only moves when the office is open.

It delivers the **view** half of the success criterion that a donor can *view and download a
receipt for any past contribution generated by their recurring gift(s) specifically*. STORY-004
delivers the download half, against the same contract.

It also delivers the single-record half of the criterion that the portal *only exposes a donor's
own contribution history, never another donor's* — **at the exact point where the donor-reachable
route already in the codebase does not**. STORY-001 defines the authorization contract and
STORY-002 enforces it on the list; this story is where ownership is enforced on a request that
names one record, which is the check the existing route omits (see Technical Notes). That is why
it carries High priority in the roster even though it adds no new data to the portal: it is the
story that closes the Epic's "and nothing else's" boundary on single-record access.

## Acceptance Criteria

[ ] Given a donor viewing their history list, when they select one of their own eligible recurring-gift contributions, then the receipt content for that contribution is displayed.

[ ] Given a contribution identifier belonging to another donor, when it is requested directly with any combination of request parameters, then the response is indistinguishable from the response for an identifier that does not exist, disclosing nothing about whether the record exists.

[ ] Given a contribution identifier that fails any scoping predicate — one-time, test-mode, or future-dated — when it is requested directly, then it is refused under that same non-disclosing contract.

[ ] Given a contribution outside the eligible-status set configured for donors, when it is requested, then it is refused under the same non-disclosing contract; and given an eligible Refunded or Cancelled contribution, the credit-note variant is rendered rather than a plain receipt.

[ ] Given a receipt is displayed, then no column of the contribution record is written, verified by comparing the record before and after the request, and no new logging or audit capability is exercised beyond what `CLR-10` authorises for the chosen route.

[ ] Given a contribution with no recorded `receipt_date`, when the donor opens it, then a defined state is shown rather than an error or a blank document, `receipt_date` is not written, and nothing is presented as the receipt originally issued unless it is verifiably that artifact.

[ ] Given a receipt is displayed, then its content is consistent with the contribution's recorded amount and receive date.

## Technical Notes

Every claim below is a **reading of source at the stated address**, obtained by static inspection
of this checkout. No runtime was stood up for this run — no CiviCRM instance was installed, no
request was issued and no data was retrieved — so nothing here rests on observed behaviour, and
any statement about what a *particular deployment* has configured is raised as a clarification
rather than asserted. The baseline is CiviCRM `6.17.alpha1` ([`xml/version.xml`:3]); a different
target version must be re-checked against these addresses before the story is estimated as it
stands.

**Why the read mechanism is forced, not chosen.** Leaving permission checks **on** looks like the
safer option, and it is not available. Core's
permission map requires `access CiviCRM` **and** `access CiviContribute` for a permission-checked
`Contribution.get` ([`CRM/Core/Permission.php`:1238-1257], the entity's `get` entry inside the
block opened at [`CRM/Core/Permission.php`:1239]). `access CiviContribute` is itself described as
conferring the ability to "Record backend contributions (with edit contributions) and view all
contributions (for visible contacts)" ([`CRM/Contribute/Info.php`:55-64], the description at
[`CRM/Contribute/Info.php`:59]). Granting a donor role that permission in order to let a donor
read their own receipt would grant it the ability to view other contributions — the opposite of
the Epic's boundary. So the read is a server-side path with `checkPermissions` set false and the
predicates appended by the server, and that follows from core's own permission map rather than
from a preference.

**State the corollary plainly, because it is the part that gets missed.** The ACL clause returns
empty the moment permission checks are off ([`Civi/Api4/Query/Api4SelectQuery.php`:336-338],
inside `getAclClause()` at [`Civi/Api4/Query/Api4SelectQuery.php`:335]). Two things a
permission-checked read would otherwise inherit are therefore never composed at all:

- the **generic Contact ACL subquery** the DAO contributes for any `contact_id` field keyed to
  Contact ([`CRM/Core/DAO.php`:3186-3207], the contact-keyed clause at
  [`CRM/Core/DAO.php`:3191-3195]) — which would in any case resolve to the caller's *visible
  contacts* rather than to self-only, so it is not a substitute for predicate 1; and
- the **financial-type filter** `financialacls` injects through the select-where hook
  ([`ext/financialacls/financialacls.php`:87]), which is dispatched only while that ACL clause is
  being built ([`CRM/Core/DAO.php`:3206]).

Any financial-type restriction on donor receipts must therefore be applied by this path itself.
That is `CLR-05`, and it is the reason the flag is classified blocking rather than informational.

Record authorization adds nothing here either: for a get action `checkAccessRecord()` re-runs the
request as a permission-checked get and returns before dispatching its listener, because
`authorizeRecord` does not consistently check permissions for `get`
([`Civi/Api4/Utils/CoreUtil.php`:253-265], the short-circuit at
[`Civi/Api4/Utils/CoreUtil.php`:257-262]). There is no second layer to fall back on.

**The non-disclosing response contract.** Every path that accepts a contribution identifier
returns **one identical response** for all six of the following:

1. an identifier that does not exist;
2. an identifier the donor does not own (predicate 1);
3. an identifier whose recurring link is null (predicate 2);
4. an identifier flagged as test-mode (predicate 3);
5. an identifier dated in the future (predicate 4);
6. an identifier outside the eligible-status set configured for donors (`CLR-13`).

A distinct "forbidden" response for an unowned record would confirm that the record exists, and
would let a caller who varies the identifier map the contribution table by response class alone —
so the refusal must not vary, must not vary in timing detail a caller can read, and must not
distinguish "not yours" from "not there".

This matters more here than it usually would, because **the route already in the codebase fails
exactly this way**: its access check validates the caller's `cid` and never the requested
contribution's ownership, as set out next. The contract is stated once here and asserted by
criteria 2, 3 and 4 above; STORY-004 asserts the same contract against the identical six-case
list, which is what keeps the viewing and download paths from diverging.

**The existing donor-reachable route, and its ownership gap.** This is not greenfield work. Core
declares `civicrm/contribute/invoice`, dispatching to
`CRM_Contribute_Form_Task_Invoice::getPrintPDF` behind the access callback
`CRM_Core_Permission::checkDownloadInvoice` ([`CRM/Contribute/xml/Menu/Contribute.xml`:299-307],
the page callback at [`CRM/Contribute/xml/Menu/Contribute.xml`:302] and the access callback at
[`CRM/Contribute/xml/Menu/Contribute.xml`:303]).

That callback resolves the session contact and returns true if the caller holds
`access CiviContribute`, **or** holds `view my invoices` **and** the request's `cid` equals the
session contact ([`CRM/Core/Permission.php`:1859-1874]). It never verifies that the
separately-supplied contribution `id` belongs to that contact — no contribution lookup occurs in
the function at all. Read plainly, a donor holding `view my invoices` could pass their own `cid`
alongside another contribution's `id`, which is precisely the failure the Epic's "and nothing
else's" exists to prevent.

The mismatch is documented in the function's own docblock, which is what makes this a defect
rather than a design choice: it states that the permission holds where "the invoice author is the
current user" ([`CRM/Core/Permission.php`:1859-1865]), an ownership semantic the code beneath it
does not implement.

> **Evidence standard.** The two preceding paragraphs are a **reading of the access callback's
> source**, not an exploit that was executed. No runtime was stood up for this run, no request
> was issued and no data was retrieved. Confirm it against the target deployment before treating
> it as an incident rather than as a design input to this story.

The surface has the shape it does because the two parameters are independent. The legacy
dashboard renders the link as `reset=1&id=$id&cid=$contact_id`
([`templates/CRM/Contribute/Page/UserDashboard.tpl`:43-60], the parameter assembly at
[`templates/CRM/Contribute/Page/UserDashboard.tpl`:48]) — `id` and `cid` travel separately, and
only one of them is checked. It renders that link when the `invoicing` setting is on and the
dashboard option is enabled, assigning a display flag from `view my invoices` **or**
`access CiviContribute` ([`CRM/Contribute/Page/UserDashboard.php`:119-146], the setting test at
[`CRM/Contribute/Page/UserDashboard.php`:126] and the permission flag at
[`CRM/Contribute/Page/UserDashboard.php`:143]).

`view my invoices` is a purpose-built donor-scoped permission, described as allowing users to
view or download their own invoices ([`CRM/Core/Permission.php`:958-961]). It is the
least-privilege candidate for a donor role, and STORY-001 owns that choice under `CLR-02`.

**Three properties of that route that correct assumptions it would be natural to make.** Each of
these changes what reusing it would actually commit the Epic to.

*It persists.* `getPrintPDF` ([`CRM/Contribute/Form/Task/Invoice.php`:671-677]) calls `printPDF`
([`CRM/Contribute/Form/Task/Invoice.php`:250]), which writes a stored file through `putFile`
([`CRM/Contribute/Form/Task/Invoice.php`:487], [`:501`], [`:514`], defined at [`:664`]) and
records an Activity through `addActivities` ([`:619`], the `Activity.create` call itself at
[`:649`]). A donor read on that route is therefore not side-effect-free today. Whether those
existing side effects are acceptable for a donor read is part of `CLR-10` and is **not** settled
here — this story asserts only that no column of the contribution record is written, which is a
different and narrower promise.

*It applies no status validation.* The four-status check — Completed, Pending, Refunded and
Partially paid — belongs to the form's `preProcess`
([`CRM/Contribute/Form/Task/Invoice.php`:67], the status set at [`:81`] and the bounce at
[`:92`], across [`:79-92`]). The donor entry point never invokes it: `getPrintPDF` retrieves `id`
and `cid` and calls `printPDF` directly ([`:671-677`], the call at [`:676`]). What `printPDF`
does instead is render Refunded and Cancelled as a credit note ([`:286-289`], the credit-note
assignment at [`:318-319`] and the template parameter at [`:410`]).

*The `invoicing` setting does not gate it.* **This claim is bounded to two files, and to those
two only:** the string `invoicing` appears nowhere in the route declaration
([`CRM/Contribute/xml/Menu/Contribute.xml`]) and nowhere in the task class
([`CRM/Contribute/Form/Task/Invoice.php`]). Within that bound, the setting governs only whether
the legacy dashboard *displays* a link ([`CRM/Contribute/Page/UserDashboard.php`:126]), not
whether the route authorizes or renders. No claim is made about the setting's effect elsewhere in
core. The consequence for the backlog is that `CLR-07` is a question about receipt shape and link
discoverability, **not** about route reachability.

**Donor status eligibility is an open product question, not an inherited rule.** The staff invoice
form's set is the only in-core precedent ([`CRM/Contribute/Form/Task/Invoice.php`:79-92]), and it
sits on a method the donor path never reaches, as above — so nothing in core currently constrains
which statuses a donor may receipt. That decision is `CLR-13`. Two invariants hold whichever set
is chosen, and both are asserted by criterion 4: the set is enforced **identically** by the
viewing path and by STORY-004's download path, and credit-note rendering is preserved for
Refunded and Cancelled rather than replaced by a plain receipt. List *visibility* of Failed and
Cancelled records is a separate decision belonging to STORY-002 under `CLR-11`.

**`receipt_date` records a send, not a document.** The column is nullable and its own description
reads "when (if) receipt was sent. populated automatically for online donations w/ automatic
receipting" ([`schema/Contribute/Contribution.entityType.php`:356-370], the description at
[`schema/Contribute/Contribution.entityType.php`:360]). Two consequences follow, and conflating
them is the most likely design error in this story:

- A recorded send is **not** evidence that a receipt document exists, and the absence of a
  `receipt_date` is **not** evidence that no receipt was ever issued. STORY-002's list labels the
  value as the date a receipt was sent for exactly this reason.
- The Contribution record stores **no canonical receipt document**. Historical PDFs may
  nonetheless exist as File records attached to Activities, because the staff path writes them
  ([`CRM/Contribute/Form/Task/Invoice.php`:664], [`:619`]) — so whether an originally issued
  artifact can be *identified as such* is `CLR-09`, an investigation, rather than a settled
  impossibility.

Criterion 6 holds under either answer: a defined state is shown, `receipt_date` is not written,
and nothing is presented as the originally issued receipt unless it is verifiably that artifact.

**The APIv3 `Contribution.sendconfirmation` action must not be used as a read path.** It is the
obvious-looking way to obtain receipt content and it mutates the record. Its `receipt_update`
input defaults to **true** when the caller omits it ([`api/v3/Contribution.php`:385-404], the
default at [`api/v3/Contribution.php`:397]), and the BAO mail path it calls
([`CRM/Contribute/BAO/Contribution.php`:3055]) writes `receipt_date` when the column is empty
([`CRM/Contribute/BAO/Contribution.php`:3068-3072]). Criterion 5 depends on no write occurring,
so this action is prohibited on the donor path — including with `receipt_update` passed false,
since a read path that is one omitted parameter away from mutating the record is not a read path.

**Three receipt shapes exist, and `CLR-06` chooses between them.** They are
[`CRM/Contribute/WorkflowMessage/ContributionOnlineReceipt.php`],
[`CRM/Contribute/WorkflowMessage/ContributionOfflineReceipt.php`] and
[`CRM/Contribute/WorkflowMessage/ContributionInvoiceReceipt.php`], each backed by templates under
`xml/templates/message_templates/`. The invoice-shaped output additionally depends on settings
that are **opt-in**: `invoicing` ([`ext/civi_contribute/settings/Contribute.setting.php`:46])
carries `'default' => 0` ([`ext/civi_contribute/settings/Contribute.setting.php`:52]), which is
what makes it opt-in rather than merely configurable, and `invoice_prefix`
([`ext/civi_contribute/settings/Contribute.setting.php`:62]) and `invoice_is_email_pdf`
([`ext/civi_contribute/settings/Contribute.setting.php`:118]) sit alongside it. So the invoice
shape cannot be assumed available on the target instance — that is `CLR-07`.

**Extend the existing coverage rather than duplicating it.** The rendered donor invoice link,
including both `id` and `cid`, is already asserted by
[`tests/phpunit/CRM/Contact/Page/View/UserDashBoardTest.php`:79-120] (the asserted markup at
[`tests/phpunit/CRM/Contact/Page/View/UserDashBoardTest.php`:120]), and invoice rendering is
covered by [`tests/phpunit/CRM/Contribute/Form/Task/InvoiceTest.php`:22-224]. The non-disclosure
cases in criteria 2 to 4 are new coverage with no existing home and need a test that varies the
identifier while holding the session fixed. Suites are declared over `tests/phpunit/api`,
`tests/phpunit/CRM` and `tests/phpunit/Civi` ([`phpunit.xml.dist`], the suites at
[`phpunit.xml.dist`:17-27]), bootstrapped through `tests/phpunit/CiviTest/bootstrap.php`
([`phpunit.xml.dist`:14]).

**No declarative mechanism is established for rendering a single receipt.** Nothing in this
checkout demonstrates Afform or SearchKit rendering one receipt document or serving a secure
single-document download; SearchKit's file-producing actions export *result sets* and refuse a
display whose `actions` setting is off — the condition these stories deliberately keep off
([`ext/search_kit/Civi/Api4/Action/SearchDisplay/Download.php`:31-34],
[`ext/search_kit/Civi/Api4/Action/SearchDisplay/SaveFile.php`:65]). This story therefore permits
**the smallest surface the capability actually needs** — a row-level link reaching a
purpose-scoped route, template or controller — rather than asserting a declarative mechanism that
has not been shown to exist, and rather than commissioning a component larger than the read
requires. Its shape follows from `CLR-10`, since hardening the existing route and specifying an
independent one imply different surfaces. Any new route must sit under `civicrm/…`, per the
routing constraint in STORY-001's contract.

**Specification anchors.** F-004 (CiviContribute), F-016 (Afform), F-017 (SearchKit) and F-023
(Financial Access Controls), together with §6.2.2. Feature identifiers take the form `F-XXX` and
requirement identifiers `F-XXX-RQ-YYY`. These anchors point at the ingested Technical
Specification, not at a file in this repository.

**Open questions.** Seven of the Epic's thirteen clarifications are owned by this story — more than
by any other, because the receipt semantics, the eligibility rule and the reuse-or-replace decision
all land here. Five are classified **blocking** for the later implementation run (`CLR-05`,
`CLR-08`, `CLR-09`, `CLR-10`, `CLR-13`): architecture, authorization or observable behaviour cannot
be settled without them. Two are **informational** (`CLR-06`, `CLR-07`) — a defensible default
exists, so implementation may proceed while the answer is confirmed, which means *proceed and
confirm*, not *ignore*. [`epics.md`](epics.md) §7 is where that classification is authoritative.

None of the seven is answered here. Each question is reproduced in full, in the same words the
closing summary uses, so that neither document can drift from the other:
